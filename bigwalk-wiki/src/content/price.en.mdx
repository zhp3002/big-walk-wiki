Here is the full pricing picture and how to find the best deal.

## Big Walk Price: What We Know

- **PC / Mac (Steam)** — **$19.99 USD** (confirmed via the Steam store listing and SteamDB pricing history).
- **PS5** — was **free at launch for PS Plus members**; check the PS Store for the current listing now that the launch window has passed.
- **Nintendo Switch 2** — paid purchase on the eShop; regional pricing varies, so check your region's store.
- **Xbox** — not available (no Xbox version exists).

Regional currencies convert from the $19.99 USD baseline, and stores set their own local figures — the Steam page shows your region's price automatically. Big Walk is developed by House House and published by Panic; any pricing news would come through those official channels.

## PS Plus: The Best Value Path

If you owned a PlayStation 5 and subscribed to PS Plus, Big Walk was **free during the launch window**. You claimed it through the standard PS Plus redemption flow rather than buying it outright. This made the PS5 the cheapest platform by a wide margin — effectively zero extra cost on top of a subscription you may already have had. Note the "already had" part: PS Plus is itself a paid subscription, so the game is free only in the sense of no additional purchase on top of it.

Two things about that offer remain **to be confirmed**: the specific PS Plus tier required (Essential, Extra, or Premium), and whether the free offer is still running now that launch has passed. Subscription offers like this are usually windowed, so if you are reading this after August 2026, check the PS Store listing directly before assuming the free claim is still there.

For groups, the launch offer had a side effect worth remembering: it lowered the barrier for the PS5-owning friends in a crossplay group. A free copy on one platform and paid copies on the others still end up in the same session.

## Price Versus Value: How Much Big Walk You Get

![Characters gathered around a glowing campsite on a dark hillside at night](/images/topics/night-camp.jpg)

Whatever the sticker price, it helps to know what a purchase covers. At $19.99, Big Walk is a finite, hand-crafted campaign: the main story runs about 11 hours and full completion about 20 (see our [length guide](/en/topics/how-long-to-beat)), with the intended rhythm of about one night a week for roughly a month. There is no battle pass, no seasonal treadmill, and no designed-in reason to keep paying after the walk ends.

That makes the per-hour math unusually legible. Take the $19.99, divide it across the weeks your group will actually play, and compare that against what a night out costs — under a dollar per person per session for a typical group. And since the campaign is played together, everyone in the group is getting those same hours out of their own copy.

## Choosing A Platform Based On Price

If price is your main criterion and you have the hardware options, the decision is straightforward:

- Have a PS5 and PS Plus? Check whether the **free offer** is still claimable, and take it if so.
- PC or Mac only? Budget for the **$19.99 Steam price** and watch for a discount.
- Switch 2 only? Check your region's **eShop listing**.

Crossplay means the cheapest platform for each person in your group can still all play together — a free PS5 copy and a friend's paid Steam copy join the same session. Price your way in separately; walk together.

Do the group math before anyone buys. Big Walk is online co-op with every player on their own device, so a group of four needs four copies across however many platforms — there is no one-purchase shared-screen option to fall back on. Budget per person, not per household, and let each member grab their own cheapest door in.

## Avoiding Fake Deals

A newly launched co-op hit attracts sketchy listings. Stick to the official stores and the legitimate trackers above, and avoid unverified third-party key sellers advertising implausibly low prices for Big Walk. If a deal looks too cheap, verify the key is legitimate before paying — and remember that the confirmed free path, where it applies, already beats every gray-market "deal" on offer.

The same caution applies to "confirmed price" articles and social posts. Launches generate confident-sounding numbers that no store page backs up. Your rule of thumb: if you cannot see the figure on the Steam page, the PS Store, or the Switch 2 eShop yourself, it is not confirmed — it is a rumor with a price tag on it.
