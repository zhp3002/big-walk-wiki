This is the key detail: Big Walk is a **Nintendo Switch 2** title. It does not run on the original Nintendo Switch. ## Crossplay On Switch 2

Big Walk supports **crossplay**, so Switch 2 players are not isolated from friends on other consoles. A Switch 2 player can join the same session as friends on **PC, Mac, and PS5**. This is especially important for Big Walk because the game is co-op only — you cannot play solo — so being able to mix platforms means your group is never split by hardware.

The session **host** owns the save file. Decide within your group who hosts the ongoing campaign; anyone on any supported platform can join. If your group's most reliable player is on PC or PS5, the Switch 2 players can still be full participants in that campaign — host choice is about who keeps the save, not who owns which console.

## How Many Players On Switch 2

Like every platform, Switch 2 supports **2 to 12 players**. The host picks a group-size mode — **2, 3, or 4+** — when starting a session, and the puzzles adjust slightly to stay solvable for that headcount. House House frames 2 players as a focused co-op experience and 12 as "an absolute mess" (lovingly).

You need at least one friend online to play. There is no single-player mode on Switch 2 or anywhere else — a deliberate design choice, since the puzzles are built to require more than one person and to require communication.

## Proximity Voice Chat Considerations

Big Walk's signature mechanic is **proximity-based voice chat**: voices fade with distance, muffle through walls, echo in tunnels, and crackle over walkie-talkies. House House recommends using the in-game voice chat rather than a separate call — the constrained audio is central to the puzzles, and many challenges deliberately separate players with soundproof walls or long distances so you have to invent your own signals with flashlights, laser pointers, and gestures.

Switch 2 players should make sure their voice setup (headset, mic) is comfortable for longer sessions, since the whole game revolves around talking to each other under constraints. Reviewers spent roughly **20 hours** per group, so equipment comfort is a real consideration.

## Starting Sessions On Switch 2

The flow for a Switch 2 host is identical to every other platform. The host hits **Host Game** on the main screen, creates or chooses a save file — the name can be anything and is always editable — and then selects how many people are playing: **two, three, or four-plus**. Switching between group sizes later does no harm to the save, which is useful when someone misses a session.

Saving is automatic and only really concerns the host: exit the game and progress continues next time. The catch is that the save file lives with the host, so the rest of the group can only continue when the host is online and running that session. Choose the person with the most reliable schedule, not the most powerful console.
