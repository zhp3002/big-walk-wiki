Big Walk reviews are **strongly positive**. Critics from IGN, Kotaku, GameSpot, and Polygon have all praised it, with at least one calling it **"one of the best games of 2026."** The praise centers on a single idea: Big Walk turns communication itself into a puzzle, using proximity voice chat and constrained tools in a way no other co-op game does. The verdicts are in since the game launched on August 4, 2026 — and if you are deciding whether Big Walk is worth playing, the critical consensus is an emphatic yes, provided you have friends to play it with.

## What Critics Are Saying

The critical throughline across Big Walk reviews is that its **communication-as-puzzle design is genuinely novel**. Reviewers describe moments of laughter, discovery, and even emotional impact that emerge naturally from trying to describe what you see to teammates who cannot see or hear you. The proximity voice chat — where voices fade across distances, muffle through walls, echo down corridors, and crackle through walkie-talkies — is repeatedly cited as the mechanic that makes the whole thing work.

Several reviewers highlight the ending as a high point, calling it unexpectedly emotional and worth the journey. The consensus is that Big Walk is a hand-crafted co-op adventure best played start-to-finish with a consistent group of friends. Two outlets independently reported spending about 20 hours with the game and still having puzzles remaining, which tells you how substantial the campaign is — this is not a small evening-length co-op lark.

## IGN's Take

IGN's review praises Big Walk as **a clever co-op puzzle adventure that uses communication like no other game**. The reviewer spent nearly 20 hours across several sessions with a core group and highlighted how the game pushes players to develop their own shared language — naming the world's unnamed objects and building communication systems unique to their group. Because so many things in Big Walk ship without names, every group is forced to invent its own vocabulary, and that vocabulary becomes something only your group understands.

IGN frames this as the game's genius: the puzzles are clever, but the deeper pleasure is deciphering the world's logic in a way that only you and your companions will share. The proximity chat is described as essential to the experience, and the review argues that Big Walk would not be the same without it. The reviewer's conclusion is that relationships and language are the real superpower here — the world does not revolve around one player, but around the group.

## Kotaku's Take

![A stack of characters climbing a tall scaffolding tower against a blue sky](/images/topics/tower-stack.jpg)

Kotaku's review is effusive, calling Big Walk **one of the best games of 2026** and emphasizing the joy of its emergent, goofy moments. The reviewer engages directly with the "friendslop" label often applied to friend-group co-op games and rejects it for Big Walk, describing the game instead as clean, earnest, and even loving — something that kept making the reviewer tear up when recalling moments from their own playthrough.

Kotaku's key argument is that Big Walk is **at its best when you and your friends are "very stupid" together** — the laughter comes from what the game facilitates between people, not from any scripted event. The cited moments are telling: a friend singing off-key into the walkie-talkie, players picking each other up and chucking each other off tall towers, and a group kicking a lawn chair around instead of appreciating a discovery. One instruction stands out as the review's most practical advice: **use the in-game proximity voice chat, not a separate Discord call**, because the constrained audio is the point — a Discord call "takes away a shocking amount" of the game's impact.

The review also praises the puzzle design for making a group feel like a collective genius, celebrating every solved puzzle with a party-horn honk and a burst of confetti.

## GameSpot And Polygon's Takes

GameSpot published a tips guide calling Big Walk **one of the best games of 2026**, framing it as an open-world co-op puzzle-adventure with few rules and no quest markers — part of the fun is getting lost and exploring. The same outlet's reviewer noted that you do not need to finish every single puzzle to reach the credits, but you will need almost all of them, and that the ending is one of the best parts of the game.

Polygon's coverage focuses on the ending, describing it as unexpectedly emotional and **a remarkable capstone that elevates a carefree multiplayer adventure into something genuinely affecting**. The piece explains why: the finale strips away the tools you have relied on all campaign, removing your ability to hear one another and washing away the colors that distinguish players, so the last puzzles can only be solved with the trust and body language your group has built over dozens of hours.

## The Reviewers' Context

The verdicts carry weight because of how they were earned. IGN's review is based on nearly 20 hours across several sessions with a core group of three; Kotaku's on about 20 hours with a larger crew of six; GameSpot's writer had beaten the game before publishing day-one tips; Polygon's ending piece came from a full playthrough alongside two friends. These are not preview impressions — they are completion-length assessments from four separate friend groups, published in the days around the August 4, 2026 launch.

The consistency across those different group sizes matters too. A three-player group and a six-player group are effectively playing different games in terms of chaos, and both came away calling Big Walk one of the year's best.

## The Shared Criticism: Movement

The one consistent criticism across reviews is that **the walking itself can be finicky**. Specifically, moving uphill or over uneven, rocky terrain sometimes gets your character stuck or wedged. Kotaku's reviewer — whose sole listed dislike was walking on uneven ground — describes getting wedged between two ordinary rocks, being unable to climb a knee-high boulder, and failing to fit through a person-sized gap while carrying a whiteboard, multiple times per session.

Reviewers note this is frustrating in the moment but quickly forgotten once something new grabs your attention. It is a minor blemish on an otherwise beloved game, not a dealbreaker. No review treats it as more than a recurring annoyance.

## What This Means For You

The reviews tell a clear story: Big Walk is a critically acclaimed co-op game that delivers a unique, communication-driven experience — but it has two non-negotiable requirements. First, you need a **group of friends** willing to commit to a start-to-finish campaign over several sessions. Second, you should **use the in-game voice chat**, because the constrained audio is the core mechanic.

Meet those conditions and you are likely to have one of the standout co-op experiences of 2026. Miss them — playing with strangers, or routing audio through Discord — and you will still finish the game, but you will have blunted exactly the thing critics loved about it.

## Summary

Big Walk reviews are **strongly positive** across IGN, Kotaku, GameSpot, and Polygon, with critics calling it one of the best games of 2026. The praise centers on its novel communication-as-puzzle design, the proximity voice chat, and an unexpectedly emotional ending that tests everything your group has learned. The only recurring criticism is finicky movement on uneven, rocky terrain. It is a must-play co-op experience — as long as you bring friends and use the in-game audio.
